# Trust and Control in Semi-Autonomous Vehicles

An individual qualitative research project by **Raphael Ugboko**, completed in Dr. Emma Dixon's qualitative research methods class at Clemson University, Fall 2025.

**[Read the portfolio case study](https://ralphugboko.dev/projects?discipline=school-projects&project=sav-trust-control)**

## Research question

How do drivers make sense of vehicle communication, negotiate control and develop trust in consumer driving-assistance features?

I designed and conducted two semi-structured interviews with Tesla drivers and used inductive reflexive thematic analysis to interpret their accounts. The study developed three themes: perceived system competence, negotiated control and communication in context.

## My contribution

This was an individual course project. I developed the research questions and interview guide, recruited and interviewed participants, worked through the transcripts, developed themes and wrote the final paper. My researcher perspective and the study's limitations are part of the method.

## Method and scope

- Two adult drivers recruited through convenience sampling.
- Zoom interviews lasting approximately 30 minutes each.
- Interviews recorded, transcribed and checked for accuracy.
- Initial coding, refinement, comparison across interviews and interpretive theme development informed by Braun and Clarke's reflexive thematic analysis.
- An exploratory account of these participants' experiences, not an estimate of the prevalence of preferences across drivers.

The study did not test driving performance, measure cognitive load in a task, compare interface prototypes or establish safe control-transition timing. Design directions are questions for further evaluation.

## Materials and participant confidentiality

Public materials present paraphrased themes without participant quotations or personal disclosures. Interview records and the full course paper remain private because the study introduction promised confidentiality and academic use. The public summary is a portfolio synthesis of that coursework, not a published or peer-reviewed paper.

The theme map is a portfolio diagram summarizing the final paper's three themes. It is not an original coding artifact or a causal model. The original final paper was completed December 10, 2025; this public materials package was prepared October 23, 2026.
