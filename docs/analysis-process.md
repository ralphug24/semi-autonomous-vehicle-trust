# Analysis process

## Study framing

The proposal asked about communication modes and preferences for transitions of control. The final analysis developed a wider account of how participants connected perceived competence, control and communication to trust. Research questions can evolve through qualitative analysis when that development is made explicit.

## Interviewing and preparation

I used semi-structured questions and probes about experience, communication, trust, control transitions and accessibility. Participants could describe remembered events and reason through hypothetical scenarios. Interviews were recorded, transcribed and checked for accuracy.

## Coding and theme development

The final paper describes this process:

1. Read the transcripts and become familiar with the accounts.
2. Generate initial codes close to participants' language.
3. Refine these into more analytic codes as patterns become clearer.
4. Compare, group and interpret codes across the two interviews.
5. Develop themes that explain relationships in the accounts.
6. Write the findings with attention to research questions, researcher perspective and study limitations.

The final paper includes Table 1, a codebook of eight codes with definitions and supporting quotations. The original final paper retains the complete table as supplied. This guide summarizes the reported process; it does not introduce a new coding exercise or claim to preserve a version history of the analysis.

The documented codes cover predictive sensing, emergency intervention, immediate takeover, manual override, visual situational awareness, context-dependent alerts, status updates and onboarding. Read these as interpretations of participant accounts rather than verified claims about vehicle safety.

## What makes a theme useful

A topic names what a participant discussed, such as a display or a warning. An interpretive theme explains a pattern of meaning, such as how communication supports confidence by making system behavior understandable. The three final themes offer that higher-level interpretation of the accounts.

The analysis was reflexive: the researcher actively developed the interpretation. The study did not report an inter-rater agreement exercise or establish that another analyst would produce identical themes.

## Reflexivity

My human-centered computing and accessibility background shaped my attention to transparency and control. My positive expectations about vehicle automation could also influence what I noticed. Reflecting on those assumptions helps explain the analytic lens; it does not eliminate subjectivity.

## Scope of the evidence

The themes are grounded in accounts from two experienced users of consumer driving-assistance features. They do not represent all drivers, establish thematic saturation or verify safety performance. Hypothetical preferences can reveal expectations but do not predict behavior under stress. Broader recruitment and scenario-based observation would be suitable next steps.
