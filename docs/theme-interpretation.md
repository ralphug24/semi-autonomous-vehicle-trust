# Theme interpretation

The accounts below are paraphrases of the course findings. They contain no participant quotations or personal disclosures. They distinguish research interpretation from proposed design work.

## Perceived competence

**Accounts:** Participants connected confidence in assistance to how they understood its sensing and features, while wanting clearer explanations of capabilities and limitations.

**Interpretation:** Trust was conditional on perceived performance and understanding.

**Design direction:** Explore guided capability onboarding and evaluate whether users develop an accurate mental model.

**Boundary:** The study did not measure detection accuracy, collision avoidance or actual vehicle safety.

## Negotiated control

**Accounts:** Participants valued assistance while wanting an understandable way to resume manual control. Preferences varied with the situation.

**Interpretation:** Control was a relationship negotiated in context rather than a single preference for manual or automated driving.

**Design direction:** Explore clear operating-mode information and understandable control-transition concepts in a suitable simulation.

**Boundary:** Interview preferences do not establish safe control-transition timing or validate an intervention mechanism.

## Communication in context

**Accounts:** Participants described visual information as reassuring for routine awareness and connected sound or tactile cues to urgency and attention.

**Interpretation:** Communication helped participants manage understanding, attention and comfort.

**Design direction:** Compare communication concepts across scenarios and accessibility needs.

**Boundary:** Workload, distraction and usability were not measured in a driving task.
